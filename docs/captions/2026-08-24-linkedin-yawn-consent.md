Most meeting tools solve note-taking by adding a guest. A bot joins the call, sits in the participant list, and everyone in the room adjusts to being recorded by someone else's server.

Yawn takes the other route. It runs on your Mac. It does not join calls, mine a calendar, create tasks, or send meeting content anywhere, and it asks for an explicit consent check before recording starts.

The part that matters most is what happens to the transcript afterward. Your notes stay separate from the generated meeting note — the app never turns something you jotted down into a claim about what was said. The note links decisions and follow-ups back to the retained transcript, and outcomes it cannot support are left out rather than smoothed into a confident sentence.

It also does not hide where the work happens. The installer is 333 MB. On first launch you choose a speech model to bring down — 464 MB, or 1.61 GB for the better one. Generated notes use a separate 8.06 GB local model you can add or remove in Settings. That is the real cost of transcription that never leaves the machine, and the download page states it instead of letting you find out later.

It is an internal alpha and the page says so, right next to the three things it will not promise: no automatic sharing, no promise of completeness, no substitute for consent.

Apple silicon, macOS 14.4 or later. If you sit through meetings, take it for a run and tell me where it breaks. That is what an alpha is for.
