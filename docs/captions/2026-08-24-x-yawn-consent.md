1/ Most meeting tools solve note-taking by adding a guest. A bot joins the call, sits in the participant list, and everyone adjusts to being recorded by someone else's server.

Yawn takes the other route.

2/ It runs on your Mac. It does not join calls, mine a calendar, create tasks, or send meeting content anywhere.

It asks for an explicit consent check before recording starts.

3/ The part that matters most is what happens to the transcript.

Your notes stay separate from the generated note. The app never turns something you jotted down into a claim about what was said.

4/ The note links decisions and follow-ups back to the retained transcript. Outcomes it cannot support are left out rather than smoothed into a confident sentence.

5/ It doesn't hide where the work happens.

Installer: 333 MB. On first launch you pick a speech model — 464 MB, or 1.61 GB for the better one. Generated notes use a separate 8.06 GB local model you can add or remove in Settings.

6/ That is the real cost of transcription that never leaves the machine, and the download page states it instead of letting you find out later.

7/ It's an internal alpha, and the page says so next to three things it will not promise: no automatic sharing, no promise of completeness, no substitute for consent.

8/ Apple silicon, macOS 14.4 or later. If you sit through meetings, take it for a run and tell me where it breaks. That's what an alpha is for.
